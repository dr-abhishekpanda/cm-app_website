## Never share patient-identifiable data with AI tools
id: patient-privacy
tags: privacy, patient data, patient names, patient details, share, paste, upload, confidentiality, identifiers, aadhaar, aadhar, abha, uhid, photos, de-identify, anonymise, phi, dpdp, safe use
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Professional ethics; Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

Treat every AI chatbot as a public place. Before you paste anything, remove:
- names, initials together with dates, and relatives' names;
- phone numbers, addresses, village or ward when not needed;
- Aadhaar, ABHA number or address, UHID, IPD, OPD or registration numbers;
- exact dates of birth, admission or discharge;
- photographs of faces, identifiable wounds or documents with letterheads.

Quick de-identification: write roles instead of names ("a 34-year-old woman"), give month and year instead of full dates, crop images, and delete the hospital name if it adds nothing.

Why it matters: confidentiality is a professional duty, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 covers personal data such as health details. Many free AI tools keep what you type, and some use it to improve their models.

The Clinic Assistant refuses messages that look like they contain phone, Aadhaar, ABHA or hospital record numbers. That is a safety net, not a guarantee.

## Choosing a general AI assistant
id: choosing-ai-assistant
tags: chatgpt, gemini, claude, copilot, meta ai, perplexity, which ai, best ai, compare, free ai, paid plan
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

The major general assistants (ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude, Microsoft Copilot and Meta AI) can all draft, summarise, translate, explain and brainstorm. What matters when you choose:

- **Where you already work.** Gemini fits Google accounts (Gmail, Docs, Drive); Copilot fits Microsoft 365; Meta AI sits inside WhatsApp.
- **Checkable sources.** Perplexity, and the web-search modes of the others, show links you can open and verify.
- **Privacy controls.** Find out whether your chats are used to train models and whether you can switch that off.
- **Free or paid.** Free plans are enough to learn. Paid plans give higher limits and stronger models.

Start with one tool and learn it well before adding others. Features, plans and prices change often, so check the tool's own help pages rather than old videos.

## Setting up ChatGPT safely
id: chatgpt-setup
tags: chatgpt, openai, setup, set up, account, sign up, settings, data controls, model training, memory, temporary chat, custom instructions, personalisation, two-factor, 2fa
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda; OpenAI help pages

1. **Sign up** at chatgpt.com or in the official ChatGPT app (publisher: OpenAI). Avoid look-alike apps.
2. **Protect the account:** turn on multi-factor authentication in the security settings.
3. **Decide on model training:** the data-control settings have a switch that lets OpenAI use your chats to improve its models. Switch it off for work use.
4. **Use Temporary Chat for anything sensitive.** These chats do not appear in your history and do not update memory.
5. **Add custom instructions** (personalisation): your role, that you work in India, and how you like answers, for example "short, Indian English, say when unsure, give sources".
6. **Review Memory** from time to time and delete anything you would not want stored.

Menu names change between versions. If you cannot find a setting, search the ChatGPT help centre for its name.

## Writing a good prompt
id: prompting-basics
tags: prompt, prompting, prompt engineering, how to ask, instructions, template, role, task, context, format, better answers
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

A good prompt tells the AI five things:
1. **Role:** who it should act as ("Act as a medical educator").
2. **Task:** what to produce ("Write 5 single-best-answer MCQs").
3. **Context:** audience, setting and what you already have ("for Phase III MBBS students in India, on the cold chain").
4. **Format:** length, structure, language ("4 options each, then the key and a one-line explanation").
5. **Checks:** what to avoid or flag ("Base them on the Universal Immunization Programme; mark any item you are unsure of").

Tips:
- Show an example of the output you want.
- Ask for a draft, then improve it step by step ("make question 3 harder", "shorten the explanations").
- Ask the AI to list its assumptions and to ask you questions before it starts.
- For facts, ask for sources, then check them yourself.

## Checking AI answers before you use them
id: verify-ai-output
tags: hallucination, verify, fact check, accuracy, wrong answer, fake references, citations, errors, made up, reliable
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

AI tools can sound confident and still be wrong. They may invent references, guideline numbers, drug doses or statistics. Before you use an answer:

- **Facts:** check them against the primary source, such as the current MoHFW, ICMR or WHO guideline, the paper itself, or the drug label.
- **References:** search each title in PubMed or Google Scholar and open the DOI. Delete any reference you cannot find.
- **Numbers:** redo calculations yourself or with a validated calculator.
- **Blind spots:** ask the AI "What are you unsure about?" and "What would change this answer?"
- **Patients:** never let AI output replace clinical judgement for a decision about a patient.

## Disclosing AI use in papers, theses and assignments
id: ai-disclosure
tags: disclosure, declaration, manuscript, journal, publication, icmje, thesis, dissertation, authorship, acknowledgement, acknowledgment, elsevier, ai statement
updated: 2026-11-26
source: ICMJE Recommendations; publisher author guidelines

Journals expect authors to say how they used AI.

- **ICMJE recommendations:** AI tools cannot be listed as authors. Authors should describe AI use in the cover letter and in the manuscript: in the acknowledgements for writing help, and in the methods when AI was used for data collection, analysis or figures. Authors remain responsible for everything in the paper.
- **Publishers:** many ask for a separate declaration of generative AI use. Elsevier places it before the reference list.
- **Always check** the target journal's current instructions for authors.
- **Theses and assignments:** follow your university's policy and tell your guide.

A sample statement you can adapt: "During the preparation of this work, I used [tool] to [purpose]. I reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content."

## Build a simple web tool without coding
id: build-web-tool
tags: build app, make app, create app, website, html, no code, low code, vibe coding, claude artifacts, ai studio, lovable, github pages, cloudflare pages, host, publish, free hosting
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

Many "I want an app" ideas need only one web page: a calculator, a checklist, a quiz or a schedule.

1. **Describe it** in plain words to an AI app builder (for example Claude, Google AI Studio's Build mode or Lovable) and ask for a single self-contained HTML file that works on phones and offline.
2. **Test it** with real examples and check every formula against the guideline it comes from.
3. **Publish it free:** create a GitHub account, make a repository, upload the HTML file and switch on GitHub Pages (Cloudflare Pages works too). You get a link you can share.
4. **Keep patient data out:** the tool should not collect names or phone numbers.

Tools that diagnose, triage or calculate doses for patients can count as medical device software under CDSCO's rules. Build those only with your institution.

## Chatbot, custom GPT, RAG: what the words mean
id: chatbot-terms
tags: chatbot, custom gpt, gpts, gem, gems, project, rag, knowledge base, agent, retrieval, grounded, llm, large language model, glossary, terms, pwa
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

- **Chatbot:** any program you chat with. Menu bots give fixed answers; AI chatbots write their answers.
- **Large language model (LLM):** the AI engine behind ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and others.
- **Configured assistant** (Custom GPT, Gemini Gem, Claude Project): an AI with your instructions and files, shared by link. Users usually need their own account.
- **Knowledge-grounded (RAG) chatbot:** searches a checked set of documents first, then answers from them. This is the safer design for health information. The Clinic Assistant app works this way.
- **AI agent:** an AI that can also take actions, such as booking an appointment or updating a record.
- **PWA (progressive web app):** a website you can install on your phone and use offline.

## WhatsApp chatbots: rules and costs in 2026
id: whatsapp-bots
tags: whatsapp bot, whatsapp chatbot, whatsapp business, api, aisensy, wati, interakt, bsp, meta, group bot, cost, ai policy, ban
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Meta WhatsApp Business documents and pricing page, checked November 2026

- A WhatsApp bot needs the official **WhatsApp Business Platform**, usually through a provider such as AiSensy, WATI or Interakt, and a phone number that is not on the ordinary WhatsApp app.
- Since **15 January 2026**, Meta's terms do not allow general-purpose AI assistants (like ChatGPT) on the Business Platform. Bots limited to a business's own topics are allowed. That is why the Clinic's WhatsApp assistant answers only Clinic topics.
- Meta charges per message. Since October 2026 each business number gets 1,000 free service replies a month, then a small fee per reply. Providers add their own monthly fee.
- A bot cannot be added to an ordinary large WhatsApp group: groups on the official platform hold at most 8 participants.
- Apps that run a bot on your personal number through WhatsApp Web are unofficial and can get the number banned. Do not use them for health services.

## Health chatbots for patients and parents: safety first
id: patient-chatbots
tags: patient chatbot, parents, public, health information, health education, symptom checker, triage, red flags, danger signs, cdsco, medical device, child health app
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda; IMNCI Chart Booklet (MoHFW); CDSCO medical device software guidance

Before you build a chatbot for patients or parents:

- **Stay with general health education** from checked sources: MoHFW and NHM materials, the MCP card, WHO and UNICEF India.
- **Put danger signs first and send people to care.** For a child who cannot drink or breastfeed, vomits everything, has convulsions, is lethargic or unconscious, or has fast or difficult breathing, the only reply should be: go to the nearest health facility now, or call 108.
- **Do not diagnose, triage or give doses.** In India such software can fall under CDSCO's medical device rules.
- **Collect no names, phone numbers or child details** unless you have consent and an institution that owns the service.
- **Test before launch** with real questions and two clinicians, and review answers every month.
- **Check what already exists.** For example, MoHFW's Kilkari service sends free voice messages to pregnant women and mothers.

## The DPDP Act, 2023: what healthcare professionals should know
id: dpdp-basics
tags: dpdp, data protection, privacy law, consent, personal data, dpdp rules, rules 2025, children's data, parental consent, breach, digital personal data protection
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023; DPDP Rules, 2025 (MeitY)

The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** governs digital personal data in India. The DPDP Rules were notified on **13 November 2025**, and most duties apply from **13 May 2027**.

- Collect only the personal data you need, for a stated purpose, with a clear notice and consent.
- Children's data (under 18) needs verifiable consent from a parent or guardian.
- Keep data secure, delete it when the purpose is over, and report breaches.
- For apps and chatbots, the simplest way to stay safe is not to collect personal data at all.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Check MeitY's official text for details.

## Using AI for teaching and assessment
id: ai-teaching
tags: teaching, medical education, mcq, lesson plan, cbme, students, assessment, quiz, osce, case scenario, faculty, rubric
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

Good uses: lesson plans mapped to competencies, case scenarios, MCQs with explanations, OSCE checklists, explanations pitched at different levels, and feedback rubrics.

Ground rules:
- Check every item against the current textbook or guideline.
- Never use real patient cases that carry identifiers.
- Use AI-graded practice for learning (formative), not for final marks.
- Tell students when and how they may use AI.

Tip: ask the AI to "explain why each wrong option is wrong". Weak or ambiguous items show up quickly.

## Using AI for research work
id: ai-research
tags: research, literature search, protocol, sample size, statistics, analysis, systematic review, writing, references, pubmed, consensus, elicit, thesis
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

AI helps with: sharpening a research question (FINER, PICO), drafting a protocol outline, explaining statistical tests, writing analysis code, summarising papers you provide, and improving language.

Take care with:
- **References:** verify every one; general chatbots may invent papers.
- **Sample size and statistics:** check with validated software or the #RESEARCH-APP.
- **Data:** upload only de-identified data, and only within your ethics approval.
- **Originality:** AI text still needs your judgement, your citations and a disclosure statement.

Tools that search real papers include PubMed, Consensus, Elicit and Semantic Scholar. Dr. Panda's [#RESEARCH-APP](/research-app/) offers tested research tools that work offline.

## Translating patient education material with AI
id: ai-translation
tags: translation, translate, odia, hindi, local language, patient education, leaflet, back translation, bhashini, sarvam, regional language
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

AI can draft Odia or Hindi versions of patient education material quickly, but errors in medical meaning are common.

1. Write the English version first, in short, plain sentences.
2. Ask for a translation for a reader with primary-school education.
3. Have a native speaker translate it back into English, and compare.
4. Ask a health worker who speaks the language to read it aloud to two or three people from the community, and fix what they stumble on.

Indian-language services such as Bhashini (Government of India) and Sarvam AI also offer translation and speech tools.

## Recording meetings and making minutes with AI
id: ai-minutes
tags: minutes, meeting, transcription, transcript, record, recording, notes, summary, action points, zoom, google meet
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

AI transcription can turn a meeting recording into minutes and action points.

- Tell participants before you record, and follow your institution's rules.
- Do not record discussions about identifiable patients.
- Check names, numbers and decisions in the draft before you circulate it.

A prompt that works: "From this transcript, write minutes with attendees, decisions, action points (owner and due date) and open issues. Keep it to one page."

## Using AI with spreadsheets and data
id: ai-spreadsheets
tags: excel, google sheets, sheets, spreadsheet, formula, data cleaning, pivot table, chart, data analysis, csv
updated: 2026-11-26
source: Dr. Abhishek Panda

AI can write spreadsheet formulas, explain errors, suggest pivot tables and charts, and list data-cleaning steps.

- Describe your columns (names and types) instead of pasting the data.
- For analysis, upload only de-identified data, keep the original file untouched, and recheck results.

Example prompt: "My Google Sheet has columns Age (years), Sex (M/F) and Hb (g/dL). Write a formula for column D that shows 'Low' when Hb is below 12 for women or below 13 for men, and 'Normal' otherwise." (These are the WHO cut-offs for non-pregnant adults; use the cut-offs that fit your population.)
