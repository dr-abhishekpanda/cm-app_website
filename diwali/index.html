01One Diya Is One Neuron

An artificial Neuron does three small things. It takes some Inputs, multiplies each one by a Weight that says how much that input counts, and adds the results together with its own Bias.

Then an Activation Function turns that total into the neuron's output. Here the Sigmoid curve squeezes any total into a brightness between 0 and 1.

Try it. More oil and a longer wick brighten the flame. Breeze has a negative weight, so it dims the flame. Raise the bias and the lamp lights more easily.

Recognise the Formula? One neuron with a Sigmoid activation computes 1 / (1 + e−(b + Σ wx)).

That is Logistic Regression, the same model behind many clinical prediction tools.

02Many Diyas Make a Network

Put neurons in rows and feed each row's outputs into the next row, and you have a Neural Network. Ours has two inputs, three Hidden Layers of twelve diyas and one output: 361 weights and biases in all.

Its job is simple to state. Give it the position of a dot, (x, y), and it answers how sure it is that the dot belongs to a picture of a diya, from 0 to 1.

Tap the square to pick a dot. Light flows up the network, layer by layer, to the answer. That journey is a Forward Pass, and it is all a trained network does when it makes a prediction, which is called Inference.

Its weights start out random, so for now its answers are guesses. Train it in step 3, then come back and tap again.

Dot (0.00, 0.40). The network says 0.52, a guess.

03Teach It to Draw a Diya

Training needs examples with answers. We give the network 1,600 dots on a grid, each with a Label: gold if it belongs to the diya, violet if it does not. A rangoli starts from a dot grid too.

Each Epoch is one pass over all 1,600 dots:

  1. A forward pass gives an answer for every dot.
  2. The Loss scores how wrong those answers are. Red rings mark outright mistakes.
  3. Backpropagation works out how much each weight added to the loss.
  4. Gradient Descent nudges every weight a little to reduce it.

Press Train and watch the guesses turn into a lamp. With the steady setting, the mistakes reach zero in about 1,000 epochs.

Try a Different Learning Rate

The Learning Rate sets the size of each nudge. Too slow and the diya takes ages to appear. Too fast and the network overshoots; the mistakes bounce around instead of settling.

Test It on Dots It Never Saw

After training, tap anywhere on the drawing, including the gaps between the training dots. Getting new cases right is Generalisation, the whole point of learning. A model that only fits its own examples suffers from Overfitting.

04From 361 Numbers to Billions

A ChatGPT-style language model is built from the same parts you have just used: neurons, weights, a loss, backpropagation and gradient descent. What changes is the job and the scale.

Learns From1,600 labelled dotsTrillions of tokens of text, then further tuning with human feedback

What This Means When You Use AI at Work

It Predicts

A language model writes the most likely next words. Fluent is not the same as correct, so check clinical facts, doses and references against primary sources.

It Inherits Its Data

Patterns, gaps and biases in the training data carry into the answers. Be careful with groups and settings that published data under-represents.

It Needs Your Judgement

Use it to draft, summarise and explain, then decide yourself. Never paste identifiable patient information into a public AI tool.
